## 2. Top 5 Riskiest Botanical & Medical Claims

These 5 claims present critical physiological danger or clinical liability if read by an end-user without qualification:

1. **Bottle Gourd (*Lagenaria siceraria*, ID 75) — `uses[1]`:**
   - *Original Claim:* 'Fresh juice consumed as an Ayurvedic cooling health tonic and digestive aid TODO(verify)'
   - *Danger:* Ingesting bitter bottle gourd juice containing toxic **cucurbitacins** triggers severe upper GI hemorrhage (hematemesis), necrotizing gastroenteritis, hypovolemic shock, and fatalities. The Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR) published formal advisories warning that any bitter bottle gourd juice must be discarded immediately.
   - *Safer Action:* Mandate a strict pre-taste bitterness warning directly in the monograph.

2. **Oleander (*Nerium oleander*, ID 26) — `uses[2]`:**
   - *Original Claim:* 'Historical cardiac medicine source under strict pharmaceutical extraction TODO(verify)'
   - *Danger:* All parts contain lethal cardiac cardenolides (**oleandrin**). Ingestion of tiny amounts causes refractory ventricular arrhythmias, severe hyperkalemia, and fatal cardiac arrest. Home preparation is universally lethal.
   - *Safer Action:* Prominently label as a lethal cardiotoxin with zero therapeutic home application.

3. **Periwinkle (*Catharanthus roseus*, ID 31) — `uses[1]`:**
   - *Original Claim:* 'Traditional folk remedy where leaf infusions are taken for blood sugar regulation in diabetes TODO(verify)'
   - *Danger:* While leaf infusions lower glucose in preclinical models, whole leaves contain potent cytotoxic **vinca alkaloids** (vincristine, vinblastine). Drinking home-brewed tea causes severe bone marrow suppression (leukopenia), hepatotoxicity, and peripheral neuropathies.
   - *Safer Action:* Reclassified as 'Not Supported / Toxic Hazard'. Remove diabetes tea claim completely from monograph and add strict oral ingestion warning to toxicity note.

4. **Neem (*Azadirachta indica*, ID 1) — `toxicity_note`:**
   - *Original Claim:* 'Foliage and dilute topical preparations are safe; concentrated neem seed oil is toxic if ingested orally, particularly dangerous for infants and children TODO(verify)'
   - *Danger:* Pediatric ingestion of neem seed (margosa) oil causes acute toxic encephalopathy, intractable seizures, severe high anion-gap metabolic acidosis, and Reye-like hepatic necrosis.
   - *Safer Action:* Fully Supported by pediatric toxicology; retain prominent pediatric oral warning badge and note.

5. **Moringa (*Moringa oleifera*, ID 10) — `toxicity_note`:**
   - *Original Claim:* 'Leaves, blossoms, and green pods are nutritious and safe; the root bark contains the alkaloid spirochin, which can induce toxic cardiac paralysis and must never be consumed TODO(verify)'
   - *Danger:* The root and root bark contain **spirochin** and moringinine, potent neurotoxic alkaloids causing acute hypotension, bradycardia, respiratory paralysis, and violent uterine contractions.
   - *Safer Action:* Fully Supported by pharmacognosy data; retain strict warning distinguishing edible foliage/pods from toxic root bark.
